Mission

Build the world's first AI-powered Historical Intelligence Operating System.

HIOS is not simply a museum, encyclopedia, or educational website.

It is a living historical intelligence platform that connects artifacts, archaeology, civilizations, historical figures, languages, migration, trade, architecture, genetics, oral traditions, historical documents, scientific discoveries, geography, and AI into one interconnected knowledge system.

The platform should help users explore humanity's interconnected history through transparent sourcing, evidence, and AI-assisted discovery.

The platform should place particular emphasis on African civilizations and the African diaspora while presenting evidence responsibly. Where historical topics are debated, clearly distinguish between archaeological evidence, primary historical sources, oral traditions, scholarly consensus, and alternative scholarly interpretations.

A Strong Differentiator

One feature that could set this MVP apart is a Perspective Explorer. For many artifacts, the app could present multiple clearly labeled viewpoints—for example, archaeological evidence, museum interpretation, indigenous or descendant-community perspectives, and areas of ongoing scholarly debate. This helps users understand both what is well established and where historians disagree, making the experience richer and more transparent.

For an MVP, I would keep the initial scope intentionally small:

Launch with 100–250 high-quality artifacts rather than thousands.
Include conversational AI with source-backed answers.
Organize artifacts by timeline, geography, and collection.
Add bookmarking and personal tours.
Build the data model so additional museums and collections can be added without redesigning the app.

Starting with a focused, polished experience makes it easier to validate user interest before expanding into features like 3D models, augmented reality, and virtual museum spaces. This foundation can later grow into a comprehensive AI-powered digital museum for students, educators, researchers, and history enthusiasts.

I think that's a much bigger vision than an AI museum—and one that could be genuinely distinctive if it's built around transparent evidence, provenance, and multiple perspectives.

Rather than presenting a single narrative, the platform can become a place where users explore archaeological evidence, historical documents, oral traditions, genetics, linguistics, art history, and competing scholarly interpretations. For topics where there is active debate, the platform should clearly distinguish between broad scholarly consensus, minority hypotheses, and cultural or oral traditions, allowing users to understand both the evidence and the disagreements.

For accurate, real-world continent geography on the World Explorer map, the best fit is the Google Maps Platform connector (via Lovable Cloud's connector gateway). It gives you:

Real vector basemaps (Maps JavaScript API) with accurate coastlines, borders, terrain
Geocoding + Places for locating ancient sites by coordinates
Overlays for trade routes, migration paths, and archaeological pins on a true globe/2D projection
Alternatives if you'd rather avoid Google:

MapLibre GL + Natural Earth / OpenStreetMap tiles — free, open-source, no API key, great for historical cartography and custom styling (matches HIOS's cinematic dark aesthetic well).
react-globe.gl / Cesium — true 3D WebGL globe with accurate GeoJSON continents; ideal for the "operating system for history" feel.
My recommendation for HIOS specifically: MapLibre GL + Natural Earth GeoJSON for the 2D explorer, and react-globe.gl for the 3D globe view — both render accurate continents, are free, and let us style them in the gold/bronze/matte-black theme. Google Maps is better if you also want live geocoding/places search.
